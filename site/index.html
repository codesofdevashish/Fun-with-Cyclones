How to read a video

The 3D box

The box follows the storm from 900 hPa, near the surface, up to 500 hPa in the mid troposphere. Streamlines and drifting tracers are coloured by wind speed; strong flow is drawn brighter and thicker so the inner core stands out.

The red surface is the vortex core (high relative vorticity). Grey patches are strong ascent, where deep convection is lifting air. The floor map is shaded by the real position of the sun, so you can follow the diurnal cycle.

Vortex tilt and shear

The white line joins the circulation centre at each level. An upright line means an aligned vortex, which is a common precursor of rapid intensification; a leaning line means the vortex is tilted.

The orange arrow is the 850–500 hPa wind shear averaged 200–800 km from the centre. The compass in the corner compares shear and tilt directions in plan view.

Intensity timeline

White is the official maximum sustained wind from the warning agency (via NCEP TCVitals). Blue is the strongest 900 hPa wind in GFS near the centre, which is usually lower than the official value at 0.25° resolution.

Red shading marks rapid intensification: an official increase of at least 30 kt in 24 hours. It is never inferred from model winds.
